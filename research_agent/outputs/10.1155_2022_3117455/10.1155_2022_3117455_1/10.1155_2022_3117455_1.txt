## Executive Summary

Automated retinal vessel segmentation is critical for diagnosing vascular and systemic pathologies. Deep learning models, particularly encoder-decoder architectures, have dominated this field. This review examines how the SegNet architecture compares against standard U-Net and attention-gated networks specifically regarding the preservation of thin vessel topology and the reduction of false-negative boundary errors. Based strictly on the available evidence pool, SegNet demonstrates high quantitative performance metrics across benchmark datasets—including the DRIVE, STARE, and HRF databases—achieving strong overall accuracy, specificity, and area under the curve (AUC) [10.1155/2022/3117455]. However, empirical direct comparative evidence addressing fine-grained topological preservation and boundary error reduction relative to U-Net or attention-gated networks remains absent from the current literature pool.

---

## Evidence Synthesis

Current literature documents the application of SegNet for automated retinal vessel segmentation [10.1155/2022/3117455]. Evaluated across multiple established benchmark datasets—namely DRIVE, STARE, and HRF—the SegNet-based model yields robust performance indicators [10.1155/2022/3117455]:

*   **Accuracy:** Reaches 0.9518 on DRIVE, 0.9683 on STARE, and 0.9653 on HRF [10.1155/2022/3117455].
*   **Specificity:** Demonstrates high background and non-vessel suppression with values of 0.9804 (DRIVE), 0.9910 (STARE), and 0.9885 (HRF) [10.1155/2022/3117455].
*   **Sensitivity:** Records values of 0.7580 (DRIVE), 0.7747 (STARE), and 0.7070 (HRF), reflecting the model's capability to detect positive vessel pixels [10.1155/2022/3117455].
*   **Discriminative Performance:** Achieves high F1 scores (0.7992, 0.8369, and 0.7918), Matthew's Correlation Coefficients (MCC: 0.7749, 0.8227, and 0.7643), and AUC values (0.9750, 0.9893, and 0.9740) across the respective datasets, outperforming numerous classical methods [10.1155/2022/3117455].

Despite these reported metrics, the evidence pool lacks direct comparative data concerning standard U-Net and attention-gated architectures, as well as granular evaluations targeting thin vessel connectivity and false-negative boundary errors.

---

## Research Gap

A significant gap exists in the comparative evaluation of encoder-decoder topologies for fine retinal structures:
1.  **Absence of Comparative Benchmarks:** While SegNet's baseline metrics are established on DRIVE, STARE, and HRF [10.1155/2022/3117455], there is no concurrent evaluation within the same evidence pool directly pitting SegNet against standard U-Net or attention-gated networks.
2.  **Topological Granularity:** Existing evaluations rely heavily on global metrics (Accuracy, AUC, Specificity) [10.1155/2022/3117455] rather than topology-aware metrics (e.g., Betti matching, centerline tree error) required to assess thin vessel preservation.
3.  **Boundary Error Isolation:** The specific quantification of false-negative boundary errors—where vessel edges taper off or thin capillaries are missed—is not isolated in current SegNet performance breakdowns.

---

## Proposed Evaluation

To address the identified research gaps, a rigorous comparative study is proposed:
*   **Architectural Benchmarking:** Implement and train SegNet, standard U-Net, and attention-gated networks under identical hyperparameter configurations using the DRIVE, STARE, and HRF datasets.
*   **Topology-Specific Metrics:** Employ specialized evaluation metrics beyond standard sensitivity and specificity, including clDice (skeleton-based overlap metric for vascular structures), connectivity-preserving loss functions, and Betti number error rates to evaluate thin vessel continuity.
*   **Boundary Error Analysis:** Utilize distance-based boundary metrics (such as Modified Hausdorff Distance and boundary F1-score) to specifically quantify false-negative rates along vessel margins and terminal capillaries.

---

## Limitations

*   **Evidence Constraints:** This review is strictly limited by the contents of the provided evidence pool, which only contains evaluation metrics for SegNet [10.1155/2022/3117455] and omits empirical data for standard U-Net and attention-gated networks.
*   **Generalizability:** Performance metrics cited are restricted to the DRIVE, STARE, and HRF datasets [10.1155/2022/3117455] and may not translate directly to pathological datasets with severe retinopathies.
