### Executive Summary

Multi-task learning (MTL) frameworks—such as the DeepMT-DR method—have demonstrated significant potential in improving diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading and lesion segmentation by jointly optimizing complementary tasks like image super-resolution (ISR) [DOI: 10.1109JBHI.2021.3119519]. While current iterations predominantly focus on low-resolution fundus image super-resolution alongside segmentation and grading, expanding these frameworks to incorporate additional low-level image enhancement tasks presents a promising trajectory for future research [DOI: 10.1109JBHI.2021.3119519].

### Key Findings & Methodological Details

* **Current Framework Capabilities:** The DeepMT-DR multi-task learning method successfully integrates image super-resolution, lesion segmentation, and diabetic retinopathy grading, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in DR grading while sustaining competitive performance in ISR and lesion segmentation [DOI: 10.1109JBHI.2021.3119519].
* **Integration of Additional Enhancement Tasks:** Beyond image super-resolution, other low-level degradation corrections—specifically **image deblurring** and **image denoising**—can be natively embedded into the multi-task learning architecture [DOI: 10.1109JBHI.2021.3119519]. 
* **Clinical Transferability:** Because low-resolution (LR) medical imagery and localized lesions are prevalent across various ophthalmic and systemic pathologies, a generic MTL framework incorporating comprehensive enhancement tasks can be extended to other clinical scenarios, such as identifying minor traumatic injuries or diagnosing occult neovascular complications [DOI: 10.1109JBHI.2021.3119519].
